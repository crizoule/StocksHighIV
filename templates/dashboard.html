How to read this screen

Who makes the list

Common stocks on NYSE, Nasdaq, NYSE American, TSX or TSXV with a market cap above and listed options, excluding , with independent top-100 lists for US$1B to below US$100B and US$100B or more. Each tab is ranked by 30-day implied volatility (IV30): the annualized move the options market is pricing for the next month. Canadian companies that also trade in New York appear once, on their US line where options are deeper, tagged with their TSX symbol.

IV rank and percentile

IV rank places today’s IV30 between its low (0) and high (100) over the look-back, while IV percentile is the share of past sessions that closed below it — a stock can sit at rank 90 yet in the 60th percentile if it spent months near the middle and spiked once. The look-back is 52 weeks once enough history exists; until then each cell names the window it used, such as “13w”. The IV history column prints that window’s low, high and average under the sparkline, and history starts with about three months from AlphaQuery, growing with every daily run.

Why this IV

Structural context identifies business exposures such as commodity and memory cycles, AI/compute spending, documented AI disruption risk, and crypto activity. It comes from company profiles or linked issuer disclosures and does not assert what caused this session’s IV. Observed moves use roughly three months of completed daily closes through the IV date. Peer comparisons exclude the stock itself, require at least three other screened companies with the same exposure and identical start/end dates, and span both cap tabs. A broad move is highlighted only when the peer median rises or falls at least 20% and at least two-thirds move in that direction. This selected high-IV sample is not the whole industry; shared gains or losses do not establish hype or a common cause.

A possible event headline must name the company, describe a concrete event, include a source link and publisher, and be dated within seven days on or before the IV session. Its article body and connection to IV have not been independently verified. Upcoming earnings show date certainty: a scheduled report is potential event risk, not proof that it is driving IV. Insufficient event evidence stays “No clear catalyst found”, which does not establish that no catalyst exists, even when structural context is available; unavailable news is flagged separately, in the row and in its details. Expand a row for sources, observed dates and the peer list. IV measures expected magnitude, not the direction of the next move.

HV30 and the IV ÷ HV ratio

HV30 is historical (realized) volatility: the annualized standard deviation of the last 30 daily closes — what the stock actually did, rather than what options expect. The ratio beside it divides IV30 by HV30, so 2.0× means options price twice the movement the stock has been making, which usually points at an event ahead; below 1.0× the options are pricing less movement than the recent past.

Squeeze setup

Small float marks fewer than freely traded shares. Short interest is reported twice a month; each row’s details carry the settlement date, which dates the position itself, and the times the float and borrow feeds were retrieved. Providers do not publish their own update time for float, days to cover or borrow data. The borrow fee is what a short pays per year to hold the position, from Interactive Brokers’ daily lendable-stock file — a fee in the double digits means the position is expensive to keep, which is the pressure that forces covering, and a dash means no borrow was on offer at all. A setup is a screening flag, not a forecast.

Price charts and earnings

The price column plots closes on the timeframe picked above the table — hourly over the past week, 4-hour over the past month, daily over six months, weekly over two years, monthly over five — and a row’s details show all five at once, each with a MACD (12/26/9) pane beneath it. Under each chart, RSI(14) and today’s volume against its 20-day average flag what is moving now; both use completed sessions only, so a day still in progress never distorts them. Earnings times are market time (ET) and read “before open”, “during session” or “after close”. Yahoo pads an estimated date with a placeholder hour, so a row marked est. shows the date only.

Sources and timing

IV30 comes from Cboe delayed quotes for US listings and from Montréal Exchange option chains for TSX-only names (at-the-money IV interpolated to 30 days). Float, short interest, 52-week range, price history and earnings dates come from Yahoo Finance; stock lists from Nasdaq, OCC and TMX. Quotes are delayed about 15 minutes, so the daily run starts after the close.

How to read these signals

These readings describe different populations and time horizons. VIX measures expected volatility, put/call ratios measure trading activity, AAII surveys the next six months, and CNN combines seven indicators. CNN overlaps with VIX and options data. We show their agreement or disagreement, not an average or a market forecast. Missing and stale readings are excluded. Daily readings become stale after 4 calendar days, AAII after 10, CNN after 2. Fetch time is separate from observation time.

Market context · US

Market leverage

How to read these measures

Each card measures a different group of borrowers. FINRA and the Fed's Z.1 accounts count money individuals and firms borrow from brokers against stocks; the OFR's figures come from large hedge funds' own filings; CFTC positions are futures, which need only a margin deposit; ProShares' 3× funds show daily how much money enters bull and bear funds and how their assets split, a same-day read of leveraged retail demand rather than a count of borrowing. Monthly and quarterly releases arrive weeks after the period they describe, so each card shows the period its data covers, when that release came out, and when the next one is due. A scheduled date comes from the publisher; an estimated one follows its usual rhythm and can slip. Readings are ranked against each measure's own history: at or above the 80th percentile is marked elevated. High leverage makes selloffs sharper when it unwinds, but it can stay high for years, so these measures describe risk, not timing.

Futures · CFTC positioning

Commodities and financial futures

How to read these charts

Each chart puts the futures price (Yahoo's continuous front-month contract, weekly closes; the spot index for VIX and the dollar index) above the CFTC's weekly Commitments of Traders positions for the same market, as net contracts (long minus short) over all open interest. The blue line is the group that carries the directional view; the orange line is the group usually on the other side.

Physical commodities (Disaggregated report): managed money (hedge funds and commodity trading advisers) leads. Producers and merchants hedge what they grow, mine, process or use, so they are usually net short and sell more as prices rise; managed-money extremes are often read contrarian.

Financial futures (Traders in Financial Futures report) have no producers, and which group to read depends on the asset. In stock indices, Treasuries and crypto, leveraged funds' large net shorts are mostly basis trades (short futures against long stocks, bonds or ETFs, financed with borrowed money), not bets on a fall, so asset managers' real-money position leads. In currencies, short-term rates and VIX, leveraged funds are the speculative bet and lead. Long Treasury or fed funds futures is a bet on lower yields or rates; long a currency is a bet it rises against the US dollar; long VIX is a bet volatility rises, which usually comes with falling stocks.

The COT index ranks the leading group's net position within the last three years (0 = least long, 100 = most long); 80 or above and 20 or below are marked. Categories and markets are ordered by open interest. Positions are as of Tuesday and published the following Friday at 3:30 PM ET. Contracts without a free continuous price are listed under each section.

Search attention · USA only

Macro search concerns Beta

Beta: Google Trends samples its data, often refuses automated requests, and can revise history between downloads, so readings are frequently missing or shift. Treat this tab as experimental.

What these searches tell us

All six word searches use Google web searches from the USA only. They measure attention, not opinions or market direction. “War” can include entertainment and other non-geopolitical uses; this is a word search, not a topic classification.

Recent: the latest seven complete days are compared with the preceding 56 days within the same three-month download. At least 1.5× baseline is elevated; at most 0.75× is below baseline. Historical: the latest complete month is ranked against earlier complete months in the 2004–present download. The percentile is the share of earlier months below it, plus half of ties. At or above the 80th percentile is elevated. Each chart shows its actual available dates. Current partial months and days are excluded.

These are descriptive thresholds, not validated trading signals. Quiet searches do not imply optimism. Each download has its own relative 0–100 scale: raw values are never joined or compared across terms or views. Sparse data is excluded. Daily readings become stale after four days. Historical readings become stale if they omit the latest completed month or were fetched over 14 days ago. Recent data is collected at most daily and historical data weekly during a dashboard refresh, with a refresh due at the start of a new month. Google can revise sampled history; dated snapshots preserve what was retrieved. This panel remains separate from Fear & Greed and stock sentiment scores.
